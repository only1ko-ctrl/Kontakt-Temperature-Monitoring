Module 01
Why This, Why Now
The compliance forcing functions driving hospital urgency -- and why 2026 is the right moment to be selling this.

Three Regulatory Forcing Functions

These regulations create non-negotiable, ongoing requirements for environmental monitoring. Each one is a door-opener for your discovery conversation.

USP 797 / USP 800 -- Sterile and Hazardous Drug Compounding

Requires continuous temperature and humidity monitoring with fully auditable logs for compounding areas. USP 800 covers hazardous drugs specifically. State boards of pharmacy increasingly cite USP 797 as directly enforceable -- not just guidance.

Key buyer: Director/VP of Pharmacy, Compliance Officer

Joint Commission, CAP, AABB, FDA -- Documentation Retention

Refrigerated vaccines, blood products, lab specimens, and food service all carry excursion-documentation requirements. Retention minimums: 3 years for most categories, up to 10 years contract-based for blood and tissue products. Every department in the hospital that stores a regulated product is exposed to this.

Key buyers: Blood Bank Director, Lab Director, Compliance Officer

The Failure Modes of Manual Paper Logs

The dominant incumbent workflow is a tech walking the floor twice a shift, reading a thermometer, and writing a number on a clipboard. This system has three structural failure modes that create real liability:

Missed Overnight Readings

Nights and weekends have no rounds. A compressor failure at midnight goes undiscovered until 7 AM -- six or more hours of product at risk.

40+ Hours of Audit Prep

Per accreditation cycle, compliance staff spend 40+ hours hunting through paper logs to produce temperature history for surveyors. That time is entirely avoidable.

Lost or Incomplete Records

Paper logs get lost, left blank on busy shifts, or read incorrectly. One gap in the log during a Joint Commission survey can trigger a finding.

Why Now -- Four Timing Factors

BLE 5.0 + Cisco/Juniper Infrastructure Is Already There

Most hospitals that have deployed asset tracking or staff safety in the last 3 years now have BLE-enabled Cisco or Juniper Mist access points throughout their facilities. The historical objection -- "we'd have to run new wiring" -- no longer applies. The infrastructure to support Temp Monitoring exists in the ceiling already.

Consolidation Cycles Favor Platform Vendors

Hospitals running separate monitoring vendors for pharmacy, lab, blood bank, and dietary are actively looking to consolidate. When they do, platform vendors win. Kontakt.io's ability to serve all four departments from one tenant is a structural advantage in any consolidation conversation.

Existing System End-of-Life Is Creating Forced Decisions

Legacy monitoring systems -- often decade-old on-prem servers bolted onto nurse call infrastructure -- are hitting end-of-life. Customers facing recertification costs and server replacement are highly motivated to evaluate alternatives. This is a natural forcing function for a vendor conversation.

NIST Certification Cycles Creating Evaluation Windows

When NIST certification periods expire, health systems are already in a review mindset -- recertifying probes, auditing their current vendor, and absorbing renewal costs. That moment naturally raises the question: is there something better on the market that consolidates vendors and reduces cost? A prospect coming up on a NIST renewal cycle is already primed to evaluate alternatives, even if they haven't articulated it as a buying trigger yet.

The Core Positioning Statement

Hospitals are required to monitor temperature and humidity 24/7 -- but most still do it with clipboards. Every missed overnight reading is a liability. Every audit is 40+ hours of manual prep. The compliance obligation already exists; Kontakt.io makes fulfilling it automatic.

The Cloud App -- How Data Becomes Compliance

Entity Model

Monitor → Storage Unit → Room. Each monitor is assigned to a specific unit or room with attributes, location, and threshold config. Import via CSV for large fleets.

Alert Rules

Configure per storage unit type or per individual unit. Set email, SMS, phone call, and webhook actions. No limit on recipients. Separate hardware alerts (device offline) from temperature threshold alerts.

Reporting

Summary Report, Temperature and Alert History, Environmental Log, Calibration Report (bulk NIST export), Audit Log. Ad hoc or recurring on schedule. PDF export for auditors.

Escalation Chains

Multi-tier escalation with configurable reminder and escalation windows. No silent auto-clears -- manual resolution required. Maintenance / paused mode prevents false alerts during planned work.

Role-Based Access

Departmental boundaries: Pharmacy sees only Pharmacy, Lab sees only Lab. Super admin gets system-wide view. Permissions configurable per user and per campus, building, or department.

Kio Setup Manager

iOS and Android mobile app. Scan QR code to provision a monitor, verify real-time telemetry, confirm calibration at install. The entire deployment is managed through this app -- no IT server required.

Voice Call Alerts

"An excursion at 3AM only matters if someone picks up." This is Kontakt.io's ownable proof point on alert accountability -- the moment the product stops being a data tool and becomes a safety net.

Step 01

Excursion Detected

A monitored fridge or freezer crosses its configured threshold. The alert opens in the platform and is logged with a timestamp.

Step 02

First Line of Alerts

The existing notification path -- app dashboard, email, SMS, and phone call -- runs unchanged. An acknowledgement window opens. If someone responds, the escalation stops.

Step 03 -- NEW

Voice Call to On-Call

If nobody acknowledges, the platform calls the on-call contact directly and works down the contact list until someone answers. Loss of product is the thing customers are actually insuring against.

Why This Matters in the Sale

The most common objection in real demo calls is not "does it alert" -- it is "what happens when nobody responds to the alert?" Voice Call Alerts is the answer. It also lands as the same mechanism for Staff Safety escalation, creating a platform-level consistency story.

Why We Win
The platform thesis, seven differentiated advantages, the competitive landscape, and where we do not compete yet.

The Platform Thesis

Most environmental monitoring is sold as a vertical point solution -- a probe and a portal. Kontakt.io's wedge is that the same badge, gateway, and cloud that runs Hand Hygiene also runs Temperature Monitoring, Asset Tracking, and Staff Safety. A customer buying Temp Monitoring is buying their RTLS infrastructure. The next three use cases land at marginal cost.

Where We Win -- 8 Differentiators

Multi-Modality in One Device

Ambient temp, humidity, and two external probe inputs all on the TM Pro. Most incumbents need two separate SKUs to cover what Kontakt.io covers with one device. Simpler to deploy, simpler to manage.

Cryogenic Coverage

Pt100 RTD probe reaches -200°C -- covering ULT freezers and liquid nitrogen storage. Most point solutions stop at standard fridge/freezer ranges and cannot serve these ultra-low temperature use cases at all.

Audit-Day Workflow, Not Just Data

The Calibration Report is a bulk NIST export. The Environmental Log is filtered and date-ranged. The Audit Log shows every corrective action signoff. The product is built around what an inspector actually asks for -- not just what engineers find interesting to log.

Escalation That Ends in a Ringing Phone

Multi-tier escalation chains, no silent auto-clears, and Voice Call Alerts as the final escalation step. Designed specifically against the "the alert went out but nobody acted" failure mode that every hospital in our discovery calls has experienced.

Departmental Boundaries Are First-Class

Pharmacy, Lab, Dietary, and Pathology each run separate configurations -- distinct thresholds, units (Celsius vs. Fahrenheit), resolution code sets, and role permissions. Pharmacy's noise does not pollute Dietary's dashboard. This is not a workaround; it is built into the entity model.

BLE 5.0 + IT-Friendly

Cisco APs function as BLE gateways -- no parallel wiring run. 30-meter range, configurable TX power, OTA firmware updates. Eliminates the historical "we'd have to run new wiring" objection entirely. Optional gateway plugin for locations without Cisco APs.

Shared Platform Across Use Cases

Same Smart Badge, Portal Light, KeyCloak identity, alert platform, and mobile installer flow as Hand Hygiene and Asset Tracking. If the customer is already a Kontakt.io RTLS customer, Temp Monitoring is an incremental add on existing infrastructure -- not a new vendor.

In-Transit / Transport Monitoring

TM Pro can be mounted on carts, coolers, or transport vehicles to provide live local alarms — visual and audible — directly on the device during transit. Alerts sync to the cloud when the device reconnects to Wi-Fi. TM Pro only; requires no additional infrastructure for in-facility transport use cases.

At Less Than 1/3 the Cost of Point Solutions

One platform investment covering multiple use cases delivers significantly better economics than three or four separate point solutions with separate contracts, support teams, and renewal cycles.

Click any objection to expand the response and talk track. These patterns were drawn from live customer conversations across multiple hospital systems.

1
"Our fridges already have built-in temperature monitoring." ▼

This comes up constantly -- especially in larger systems where some equipment shipped with onboard sensors. The built-in system is real, but it is a single-unit display with no centralized alerting, no escalation chain, no audit-ready reporting, and no cross-department visibility.

Kontakt.io becomes the source of truth for all alerts and all data. The onboard sensor becomes a local backup redundancy. The key shift: centralized alerting with department-specific routing means the right people get notified at the right time, regardless of what equipment the manufacturer included.

Talk Track "That's actually common -- a lot of units ship with a local temperature display or a basic alarm. What we typically find is those systems are great for local visibility, but they don't give you centralized alerting, escalation when nobody responds, or the audit-ready reports that surveyors ask for. We sit on top of whatever you have as the platform that aggregates all of that into one place, with department-level routing so the right people see the right alerts."
2
"Our Wi-Fi is unreliable in some areas." ▼

This objection reflects a misunderstanding of how the system works -- which is your opportunity to reframe. Kontakt.io uses the BLE radio built into Cisco or Juniper Mist access points, not the Wi-Fi radio. BLE connectivity is more reliable than Wi-Fi in challenging RF environments like hospital mechanical rooms and refrigerated storage areas.

For clinics or satellite locations without Cisco APs, an optional gateway plugin device (plugs into any standard wall outlet, works with any Wi-Fi) eliminates the infrastructure gap entirely.

Talk Track "Good question -- the sensors don't actually use Wi-Fi. They broadcast Bluetooth LE, and the access points in your ceiling pick up those BLE packets. It's a different radio than Wi-Fi and generally more reliable in dense environments. For any locations where you don't have the right Cisco or Juniper APs -- like an outlying clinic -- we have a gateway plugin that works with any Wi-Fi. We'd do a survey before deployment to identify any coverage gaps and make recommendations."
3
"Some of our cryo freezers have sealed doors -- you can't run a probe wire through the gasket." ▼

This is a legitimate edge case that came up in a live discovery call. Most units accommodate a flat ribbon cable through the door gasket or have a sealed conduit port for exactly this purpose. For truly sealed units, a hybrid approach may apply -- the onboard sensor for that specific unit, with Kontakt.io monitoring everything else in the fleet.

This is worth scoping unit-by-unit during discovery. Get the model numbers and specific units where this is a concern -- it is likely a small percentage of the total fleet.

Talk Track "That's a fair point and it does come up occasionally with cryo units. Most units either have a rubber gasket that a flat ribbon cable can pass through, or they have a sealed conduit port. For the ones that truly can't accommodate a probe, we can scope that out unit by unit during deployment -- it's usually a small number, and for those specific units there may be a hybrid approach. Can you give me a rough sense of how many cryo freezers we're talking about versus the rest of the fleet?"
4
"IT is going to own this and they don't want to be responsible for it." ▼

The ownership question is the most structurally complex challenge across the customer discovery calls reviewed. Hospitals regularly try to push clinical compliance responsibility onto IT, and IT reasonably pushes back. This must be resolved before or during deployment -- not after.

The model that works: IT serves as system administrator (configuration, backend health, hardware alert routing). Clinical departments own the alert response and corrective action workflow for their specific units. This split must be explicitly established with department leaders during the stakeholder meeting and built into the training plan.

Talk Track "This is one of the most important conversations to have before we deploy. What we've seen work is a clean division: IT owns the system -- configuration, keeping devices online, routing the hardware alerts if something goes offline. The clinical teams -- pharmacy, lab, whoever owns the contents of each unit -- own the temperature alerts and the corrective action workflow. The key is making sure that's established explicitly, not assumed. We'd build that into the kickoff and training plan. Would it be helpful to bring the department heads into that conversation before we finalize the scope?"
5
"We've tried automated alerts before and people just ignore them." ▼

Alert fatigue is real — and Kontakt.io is specifically designed to address it. Five features work together to make sure every alert that fires is worth acting on:

  • 1
    Alert delay — a unit must stay out of bounds for a configurable time window before any alert fires. Door-open events and brief spikes don't page anyone.
  • 2
    Temperature-state escalation — escalation is driven by whether the fridge is still in excursion, not just whether someone clicked acknowledge. If the temperature hasn't recovered, the alert stays open.
  • 3
    Escalation that doesn't stop — if nobody responds, alerts escalate through the contact list, including voice calls. There is no silent auto-clear.
  • 4
    Right person, right unit — customizable routing means only the relevant team gets alerts for their specific units. Less noise means less fatigue.
  • 5
    Accountability trail — every notification is logged with who was contacted and when. Leadership can see who didn't respond — which changes the culture over time.
Talk Track "That's actually the design problem we built against. Most alert systems stop at 'we sent you an email.' Ours is smarter than that in two ways: first, the alert doesn't even fire unless the fridge has been out of range for a set time window — so you're not getting paged every time someone opens the door. Second, escalation is driven by the fridge's actual temperature state, not just whether someone clicked acknowledge. If the problem isn't resolved, the alert stays open. And if nobody responds, it works down the contact list until someone picks up — including a voice call. Every step is logged, so leadership has a full record of what happened and who was notified."
6
"We need daily check-ins documented -- not just alerts when something goes wrong." ▼

Several immunization programs and clinical research teams require a documented daily check-in confirming that someone reviewed the monitoring dashboard, even if no alerts fired. This is a workflow requirement beyond alert response.

Current approach: The monitoring dashboard summary report can be generated daily showing all units in range, with timestamp and user attribution -- functionally serving as the documented check-in. A dedicated one-click daily check-in workflow is on the product roadmap. Raise this requirement with the product team and your SE before making it a deal commitment.

Talk Track "That's a real requirement -- we've heard it from vaccine programs and clinical research. Right now the way customers handle it is by generating the daily monitoring dashboard summary, which shows all units were reviewed and in range, with a timestamp. It's a few clicks rather than a single button, but the audit record is there. We do have a one-click check-in workflow on the roadmap -- I want to make sure I'm setting accurate expectations, so let me get you confirmation from the product team on timing before we call that a committed feature."
7
"How long are NIST certificates stored? We have studies that run 7-10 years." ▼

Physical and electronic copies of NIST certificates are provided at time of device shipment. Certificates are accessible within the platform for the duration of the active contract. For historical certificates after a probe is replaced (on the three-year cycle), long-term archival within the platform beyond contract term is a known feature request -- customers should retain physical copies for compliance periods exceeding the contract term.

Talk Track "Great question, and especially relevant for clinical research settings. The NIST certificates ship with the devices both as physical copies and are accessible in the platform. For the long-term archival case -- if a probe gets replaced on the three-year cycle and you need the original certificate seven years later -- I want to be honest that long-term post-contract archival is on the product roadmap but not fully scoped yet. For any study requiring that level of retention, I'd recommend maintaining physical copies as the authoritative record and treat the platform as your operational access layer. I'll get you specifics from the product team."
8
"I need hardware alerts to go to IT and temperature alerts to go to clinical. Can you separate those?" ▼

Yes -- fully separable. Alert rules in the platform are entirely independent. Hardware alerts (device offline, connectivity lost) can be routed exclusively to IT or facilities. Temperature threshold alerts route to the relevant clinical department for that unit. No cross-contamination between the two alert types.

Talk Track "Absolutely -- that's actually a common setup and it's built into the alert rule configuration. Hardware alerts -- device offline, battery low, connectivity issues -- go to IT or facilities, whoever owns the infrastructure. Temperature threshold alerts go to pharmacy, lab, or whoever owns the contents of that specific unit. They're completely independent rules. You configure them separately, they route separately, and neither group gets noise from the other."
9
"We have clinics that are not on Cisco access points." ▼

This is specifically addressed. An optional gateway plugin device extends Kontakt.io coverage to any location with Wi-Fi -- it does not require Cisco or Juniper Mist infrastructure. The gateway plugs into a standard wall outlet and communicates via any available Wi-Fi network. This means clinics, satellite locations, and off-campus facilities can all be included in the same Kontakt.io tenant.

Talk Track "That's what the optional gateway plugin is for. It's a small device that plugs into any standard outlet, connects to whatever Wi-Fi network is available at that location, and acts as the BLE gateway for the temperature monitors in that space. So your main campus runs through the Cisco APs, and any clinic that doesn't have that infrastructure yet just gets a gateway. We'd do an infrastructure survey before deployment to identify exactly where you'd need them."
10
"Is there a mobile app?" ▼

There is no dedicated downloadable app for end users. The platform is cloud-based and fully mobile-responsive. When an alert fires and a text or email is sent, the message contains a direct link. Clicking that link opens the platform in a mobile-rendered browser page -- the user can acknowledge the alert, view excursion details, and mark it resolved, all without downloading anything. The Kio Setup Manager is a dedicated mobile app (iOS and Android) used specifically for device provisioning and installation.

Talk Track "There's no separate app to download for daily users -- the platform is cloud-based and the mobile experience lives in the browser. When you get a text or email alert, there's a link right in the message that takes you to a mobile-formatted page where you can acknowledge and resolve the alert. Same system, just accessed through a link instead of an app. For device installation and provisioning, there is a Kio Setup Manager app for iOS and Android that our technicians use during deployment."
Module 07
Change Management and Behavioral Change
The hidden sale. Getting a hospital to buy Temp Monitoring is step one. Getting staff to use it -- and actually abandon paper rounds -- is the sale that determines whether the customer succeeds.

The Real Friction Is Not the Technology

The sensors work. The alerts fire. The reports generate. The friction is getting a pharmacy tech who has done manual log rounds every shift for eight years to trust that the system caught it -- and to change their behavior based on that trust. That is not a product problem. It is a change management problem, and it starts with you in discovery.

Three Behavioral Changes That Must Happen

Paper Rounds to Dashboard Check-Ins

Nurses and pharmacy techs must stop walking to each unit to read and initial a log, and start checking the dashboard to confirm status at a glance. This is not just a tool change -- it is a mental model change about what "I verified the temperature" means.

Alert Ignoring to Acknowledge-and-Resolve

In every discovery call, buyers described alert culture problems -- people ignoring texts and emails. The escalation chain addresses the technical side. Building accountability culture (department heads seeing who responded and who did not) addresses the behavioral side.

Paper Binders to Digital Audit Trust

Compliance officers must trust that a machine-generated log is more auditable than a handwritten one -- not less. This is sometimes counterintuitive. Surveyors who have seen both consistently prefer continuous digital records over twice-daily paper readings.

Discovery Questions to Raise with Buyers

These questions surface the change management complexity before you close -- making you a better advisor and giving you the information you need for a realistic deployment plan.

"Who is currently responsible for temperature log compliance in pharmacy? In lab? In dietary? Are those the same person or different people?"
"Walk me through what happens when an alert goes unacknowledged today. Who is held accountable?"
"Have you ever lost product due to a missed excursion overnight or on a weekend? What happened afterward?"
"How does your team currently prepare for Joint Commission or state board surveys on temperature data? How long does that take?"
"If you installed an automated monitoring system tomorrow, what would you need to do to get your staff to actually use it?"
"Is your current system creating accountability -- meaning, can leadership see who responded to an alert and who did not?"

What Good Adoption Looks Like

At 30 Days

  • All departments have logged into the platform
  • Alert routing has been validated per-department
  • At least one excursion has been acknowledged and resolved through the system (not by phone call)
  • Escalation chain has been tested

At 90 Days

  • Paper log rounds have been formally discontinued in at least one department
  • Corrective-action codes are being used consistently
  • First ad hoc report generated for a surveyor or department head
  • Conversation started about next use case (Asset Tracking, Staff Safety, or Hand Hygiene)

Frame This as a Discovery Topic, Not a Feature List

Change management is not a slide to present. It is a set of questions to ask in discovery that help you understand how hard the adoption challenge will be -- and whether the customer has the internal champions to pull it off. The best deals include a Compliance Officer who owns the change, not just an IT sponsor who bought the software.

SDR: Prospecting and Qualification Playbook

Who to Reach

  • Director of Pharmacy or VP of Pharmacy
  • Director of Facilities or Facilities Manager
  • Compliance Officer or Quality Director
  • Ask for a warm intro from IT if that's your entry point

Ideal Profile (Quick Qualifiers)

  • Hospital or health system, 100+ beds
  • Upcoming Joint Commission or state board survey
  • Recent USP 797/800 enforcement news in their state
  • Already has Cisco Wi-Fi infrastructure (reduces complexity)

Cold Outreach Frame (Email / Phone)

"Hi [name] -- reaching out because hospitals in [region] have been dealing with increased scrutiny around USP 797 and 800 compliance on refrigerated pharmacy storage. We work with health systems to replace paper temperature log rounds with automated continuous monitoring -- it's a significant time save for pharmacy and lab staff and it produces the kind of audit-ready log that surveyors actually ask for. Are you the right person to have a quick 15-minute conversation about what your current process looks like?"

BANT Qualification Questions

  • Budget: "Is there an active initiative around compliance monitoring, or would this need to find a budget home?"
  • Authority: "Who else would need to be involved in a decision like this -- IT, Facilities, anyone on the clinical side?"
  • Need: "Are you doing manual temperature log rounds today? How often?"
  • Timing: "Do you have a Joint Commission or state board survey coming up in the next 12 months?"

Pass to AE when: 1+ clinical champion identified, manual rounds confirmed, budget conversation is open, survey timing is near term.

AE: Discovery, Demo, and Close Playbook

Discovery Conversation Structure

  1. Map the current state: What does your manual log round process look like today? Who does it, how often, on what shift?
  2. Find the forcing function: USP 797/800 compliance gap? Upcoming survey? Recent excursion? Staff time pressure?
  3. Establish the stakeholder map: Who owns pharmacy? Who owns facilities? Does IT need to approve? Who is the champion vs. the approver?
  4. Scope the fleet: How many units? Where? What building, what floor? Any cryo or specialty units?
  5. Surface the ownership question early: If alerts fire, who is responsible for responding -- pharmacy, facilities, or IT?
  6. Ask about the survey history: Have you ever had a citation for temperature monitoring deficiencies? How did you respond?

Demo Sequence

  1. Dashboard live view -- "this is what your team sees every morning"
  2. Alert configuration -- routing by unit and department
  3. Escalation chain -- voice call capability
  4. Reporting -- export the log as PDF, auditor-ready
  5. Historical data -- show the trend line for a single unit

Close Questions

  • "What would have to be true for you to move forward in the next [timeframe]?"
  • "Who else needs to be in the room before this gets approved?"
  • "If the survey happened tomorrow, how confident are you in your current monitoring documentation?"

Typical Deal Structure to Propose

Hardware: TM Pro for all units where probe placement is needed. TM Lite for ambient area/room monitoring only. Optional gateway plugins for any non-Cisco locations. Software: per-sensor cloud subscription, annual. Professional Services: deployment labor, infrastructure survey, admin training. NIST calibration: included with all sensors at ship.

SE: Technical Discovery and Demo Prep Playbook

Pre-Demo Infrastructure Questions

  • What wireless infrastructure vendor is in place? (Cisco Catalyst/Meraki, Juniper Mist, or other?)
  • Is BLE scanning enabled on the APs, or does that need to be turned on by IT?
  • Are there any dead zones -- lower floors, basements, utility corridors -- where AP density is low?
  • What is the firewall posture? Are there outbound HTTPS restrictions on the network?
  • Is there an existing IoT VLAN, or would the sensors live on the general hospital Wi-Fi network?
  • Are there any cryo or specialty units that may require probe accommodation?
  • What identity provider / SSO is in use? (For Kontakt.io cloud account provisioning)

TM Pro vs. TM Lite Decision Logic

  • TM Pro: Any unit storing controlled medication, blood, reagents, or vaccines. Any unit where a regulatory standard applies to the stored items. Any unit where the probe must enter the interior.
  • TM Lite: Ambient room / corridor temperature only. Non-regulatory spaces. Walk-in areas where interior probe placement is not needed.

Voice Call Alerts Scoping

  • Who is in the on-call escalation chain for each department?
  • What phone numbers receive voice calls?
  • Is the hospital on an internal PBX or do staff use personal mobile numbers?
  • Are there overnight or weekend coverage gaps where the escalation chain is thin?

Proof of Concept Scope Recommendation

For a technical pilot, recommend scoping to a single department (pharmacy or lab) with 5-10 units. Validates: BLE coverage with existing AP infrastructure, alert routing per department, escalation chain end-to-end (including voice call if applicable), reporting export workflow. Timeline: 2-3 weeks from hardware ship to full validation.

CSM: Onboarding and Expansion Playbook

Week 1 Onboarding Checklist

  • Confirm department admin accounts are created and verified
  • Validate alert routing per department -- send a test alert, confirm it arrives
  • Confirm escalation chain is configured and tested end-to-end
  • Walk each department admin through the corrective-action workflow
  • Confirm dashboard access for any compliance officer or department head who requested visibility
  • Set 30-day check-in on calendar before leaving kickoff

30-Day Health Check

  • Are all monitors reporting? (No gaps in the data-sync timeline)
  • Has at least one alert been acknowledged through the platform?
  • Is the corrective-action field being used?
  • Has any department head requested a report yet?
  • Any unit that has not been reporting -- investigate and resolve

Expansion Conversation Triggers

  • Customer mentions an upcoming survey -- offer to help generate the audit report as a pre-survey run
  • Customer mentions a new building, wing, or satellite clinic -- flag as expansion opportunity
  • Customer mentions a near-miss or excursion that got caught -- reinforce ROI, start the expansion conversation
  • Customer's 90-day review shows paper rounds discontinued -- propose next use case (Asset Tracking, Hand Hygiene)

The Expansion Conversation

"Now that you have the platform running for temperature, the incremental cost to add Asset Tracking or Hand Hygiene monitoring is a fraction of a new platform purchase -- you already have the infrastructure, the admin accounts, and the escalation chain in place. This is the architecture play: each use case shares the same BLE sensor network and the same cloud tenant."

Sales Leader: Pipeline and Coaching Guide

Deal Qualification Signals (Green)

  • Survey scheduled within 12 months
  • Compliance Officer is a named champion
  • IT infrastructure confirmed as Cisco or Juniper Mist
  • Recent excursion incident mentioned in discovery
  • Budget owner identified and engaged

Deal Risk Signals (Red)

  • IT is the only contact -- no clinical champion
  • Ownership question unresolved ("we'll figure it out")
  • No survey or regulatory event on the horizon
  • Non-Cisco infrastructure, no gateway plan scoped
  • Budget requires capital approval cycle (18+ months)

Coaching Questions for Deal Reviews

  • "Who owns the alert response when a temperature excursion fires? Has that been established on the customer side?"

  • "Is there a forcing function -- a survey, a recent incident -- or are we selling prevention to a prospect who has not felt the pain?"
  • "What is the expansion plan post-close? Who is the platform conversation with?"
